The binding constraint

A phone cannot reach the size FR‑9.7 asks for

FR‑9.1 requires the ล้ง to read the count from about 10 metres. The signage rule of thumb is roughly 25 mm of character height per 3 m for comfortable reading — about 83 mm at 10 m. Bare legibility threshold is near distance/250, about 40 mm. So the PRD's own 50–80 mm range spans "marginal" to "comfortable", which matters when choosing where in it to land.

In landscape the ceiling is the screen's short dimension, less margins. Work it through and the phone tops out below the comfortable figure — on every phone.

Finding F-1 · the fix that resolves F-1 to F-4

Three registers, and every element belongs to exactly one

FR‑5.1, FR‑5.2 and FR‑5.3 each ask to be prominent. At 59 mm the numeral claims the full height and about 100 mm of the phone's 143 mm width — leaving a 35 mm column for everything else. That is a strip, not a panel. The way out is that anything the other party needs but cannot read at 10 m can be heard instead of shown. FR‑11 already built that channel.

  1. Public

    the ล้ง, the worker lead, anyone standing there · ~10 m

    The basket count. Nothing else.

  2. Operator

    the person tapping · ~0.4 m, when they can look

    Pile, expected reading, running weight, sale total, controls, state.

  3. Announced

    everyone present · audible

    Read-back, พร้อมค่ะ, each count, corrections, pile totals.

Finding F-8 · PRD open question 1

Five variants for the one tap per basket

Errors made in rhythm are caught socially within a basket or two — the spoken count stops matching what the people at the scale are tracking. Errors made out of rhythm — a pocket tap, a brush while repositioning — are caught by nobody. Spend the prevention budget there, and leave in-rhythm counting frictionless.

VariantWhy it might winWhat it costs
V1 Minimum interval lockout Trivial; kills the double-tap, the most common mistap Rejects genuinely fast piles; the threshold is a guess until you have interval data
V2 Count now, prominent undo on fast repeats Never blocks a real basket; leans on the spoken count as detector Needs the operator to act during a window while their hands are busy
V3 Tap-target geometry Dead margins stop edge brushes from the grip The margin competes with the 35 mm the operator strip already needs
V4 Swipe to count Brush-resistant; no precise aim; survives gloves Slower per basket; untested on a wet or dirty screen
V5 Hardware volume key Eyes-free and tactile — and it frees the whole screen for the numeral, fixing the layout squeeze and the tap problem at once Unavailable to a web app or PWA. Needs a native wrapper

F‑9 — this reaches past UX. If V5 wins, pure‑PWA leaves the option set. NFR‑5 has not settled packaging and docs/architecture.md is unwritten, so try V5 before that decision hardens rather than after.

The seven gaps

What the PRD does not currently answer

  • F-1

    Three requirements claim prominence on a surface with room for one number.

  • F-4

    At pile change the public number falls 87 → 1 with nothing on screen to explain it. From 10 m that reads as data loss.

  • F-7

    A silently rejected tap turns a phantom count into a missed count — the more expensive error. Rejection must be as loud as acceptance, and different from it.

  • F-9

    The best-looking tap variant is unavailable on the packaging the architecture may otherwise pick.

  • F-11

    Corrections are timestamped but never spoken. Undo basket 42 and the room has heard "สี่สิบสอง" while the record says 41 — then the next count announces "สี่สิบสอง" again, which sounds like a double-count. The transparency model fails at the exact moment a suspicious counterparty is paying attention.

  • F-14

    The app is blind to the scale. A basket that is off-standard and unnoticed is recorded as standard — and the settlement then asserts, with a full audit trail, a weight nobody measured. Make ไม่ตรง a sibling of the count button, not a mode in a menu.

  • F-16

    Five states need non-colour encoding and the strip is 35 mm wide. The clock flag and sync status have not earned a place on the counting screen.

F‑11 and F‑14 look like genuine holes in the transparency model rather than matters of taste. They are the two worth deciding before epics are sharded.

Suggested sequence

One of these needs a printer, not a prototype

  1. this week · no code

    Print 1, 42, 187 at 40, 50, 60, 80 and 110 mm. Take them to your yard, in the sun, and have your ล้ง read them from where they actually stand. That settles whether the counting device is a phone or a tablet — which governs the tap work and feeds the packaging decision.

  2. then

    Build the five variants on whatever device step 1 selected. Log (sequence, timestamp, accepted/rejected, variant) in all of them, and give every one of them loud rejection feedback.

  3. at a real sale

    Set the interval threshold from the histogram of what the weighing rhythm actually was — not from a number chosen beforehand.

  4. at least once

    Run it with the volume off. If the counter is trustworthy silent, it is comfortable with sound. The reverse does not hold.
