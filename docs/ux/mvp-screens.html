mon-farm · docs/ux · MVP screen designs draft v0.1

One sale, end to end.

Seven screens covering the first MVP: set up a sale across several zones and piles, count baskets, correct mistakes without erasing them, close piles and zones, and settle — with the total broken down per zone and each side's share shown as arithmetic anyone can check by hand.

Content is a worked example, not a decision: ไร่คุณพ่อ selling cup lump to ล้งเจริญทรัพย์ at ฿26.50/kg, 60/40 owner/worker, bucket 2 kg, standard basket 50 kg. The numbers reconcile — the settlement in screen 7 is derived from the counts in screens 3–5, so it's a real check on the money chain, not placeholder text.

2

Zero check & tare, before every pile

Three steps stand between arriving at a pile and counting it: does the empty scale read zero, what does the empty bucket weigh, and a read-back of the expected reading spoken aloud. A failed zero check is flagged and recorded, never blocking — the person in the yard decides whether to carry on, not the app.

Counting — the Public register

The screen the buyer is looking at. One number, as large as the device can physically make it, and a stroke travelling the perimeter as the three-second hold completes. Press and hold the stage to try it.

2 + 50 = 52 กก. · ล่าสุด 8 วิ
PublicThe numeral and the bezel. Nothing else — this is a fixed budget and it is already spent.
OperatorBreadcrumb, timers, state pills, the expected-reading chip, and the action row. All at arm's length, none of it competing with the count.
AnnouncedEach accepted basket speaks its number in Thai — แปดสิบเจ็ด, not digit-by-digit.
Writesaccept on a completed hold, reject with the held duration on an early release. A rejection is recorded because a silently dropped tap becomes a missed count.

Close pile sits at the far end of the action row, away from undo. Destructive and irreversible controls are never adjacent to each other or to the stage, and always take a second confirm.

The three state pills top-right — muted audio, unsynced count, and (not shown here) a clock anomaly — all report without blocking. A screen that stops you counting because its clock is wrong has misunderstood which of the two matters more.

5

Log — corrections that don't erase

Every event in sequence order, including the ones that didn't count. A voided basket stays on the record, struck through, with the correction appended below it — you can see that something was changed, what it was, and why.

OperatorThe whole log. Sequence numbers carry the order, not timestamps — the device clock can't be trusted (FR-12.5).
AnnouncedCorrections are spoken too. Finding F-11 caught this gap: if a void is silent, the heard record and the stored record diverge, and the buyer's memory is of a number that no longer exists.
Never destructiveA void appends a correction pointing at a sequence number. The original event is never mutated or removed — #113 is still there, struck through.
Row #111An announcement that failed still writes an event, flagged as not heard. The log records what was attempted, which is what makes silence auditable.

Pile closed — the decision point

Closing a pile is the moment the earlier prototype handled worst: it offered a single "continue" button and left the operator guessing where it went. The alternatives are all visible, and the total is read aloud before anyone moves on.

PublicThe pile total, at title size. The buyer should be able to read the closing figure from where they're standing, not just hear it.
AnnouncedThe total is spoken before the options appear. Closing a pile is a number both sides should agree on out loud.
Four visible optionsNext pile here, switch zone, close zone, close sale. No hidden default — the previous "continue" button chose for you and told you afterwards.
Writespile_closed, announced. Reopening later appends pile_reopened with a typed reason and is announced publicly too — the mechanism that makes the original count trustworthy has to cover its own correction.
7

Settlement — total, per zone, per share

The screen the MVP exists for. Not one opaque total: the chain from baskets to each person's share, laid out so it can be checked by hand with a calculator. The per-zone figures add up to the sale total exactly — each zone is rounded once and then summed, never apportioned from a sale-wide figure, because a one-satang discrepancy in front of a buyer costs more trust than it saves effort.
