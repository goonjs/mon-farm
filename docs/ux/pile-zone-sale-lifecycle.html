What happens after you close something.

The counter screen closes a pile and closes a zone. Two questions it doesn't answer yet: where does the operator go next, and what happens when a closed pile or zone needs to change. Both are one connected journey — written here for a dev, a QA pass, and UI design to each act on directly.

Why this needs its own document

The prototype closes a pile and closes a zone, but two real gaps remain. After closing something, where does the operator go? Right now, closing the last pile in a zone dumps the operator into the raw switcher with no guidance — a sale normally moves through several zones in sequence, and eventually has to end, and neither of those is designed yet. What happens when a closed pile or zone needs to change? Nothing does — closed is permanent. Real selling doesn't work that way: a pile closed by mistake, a buyer disputing a total after the fact, a zone force-closed around a pile still mid-count. The system needs a way back in that doesn't compromise the audit trail or the trust FR‑9 is built on.

State model

Three levels, each simple on its own

They interact — closing a zone can force-close a pile, closing a sale can force-close a zone — but each level's own states are just three or four boxes.

The concrete gap

Closing a pile should be a decision, not a dead end

Right now closing a pile shows a transition card and either jumps to the next not-started pile or opens the raw switcher. There are genuinely four different things that might be true next — this should be a real decision point with three or four labelled choices on screen, not a bare "continue."

Closing a zone repeats the same shape one level up — another zone with work left, or the sale is done. Closing the sale is not yet built. Proposed: available any time, worded honestly even with little counted ("1 zone, 12 baskets, 600 kg — close this sale?"); the confirmation shows the whole-sale rollup; any zone still open with a pile still counting is named and cascaded closed, the same pattern zone-close already uses on its piles. Once the sale is Closed, it hands off to FR‑7 Settlement — a different screen, out of scope here — and, critically, no pile or zone under it can be reopened until the sale itself is reopened first. Settlement math shouldn't be able to shift under someone after it's been shown as final.

The core of this document

Reopening and adjusting

None of these are edge cases — all of them happen in a real selling season: a pile closed by mistake, a basket weighed just after the close tap, a zone force-closed around a still-counting pile, a buyer disputing a total after the fact. A system that can't handle any of these gets "corrected" on paper instead — exactly the failure this product exists to remove.

Reopening is a new event, not an undo

The original Closed event stays in the log forever, unedited. A Reopened event is appended — actor, reason, timestamp, sequence number. Closing again appends a new Closed event. Nothing is overwritten — the same append-only model NFR‑3 and FR‑12 already require, extended to cover this new kind of correction.

Friction scales with reach

Voiding one basket is a tap and a reason chip. Reopening a whole pile or zone makes an already-shown total editable again — recommend a typed reason, not a canned chip, plus a restatement of consequence before a second, deliberate confirm.

A reopen the ล้ง watched close is a transparency event, not just data

FR‑9's whole thesis is that the count is trustworthy because it was watched happening. Silently reopening and recounting breaks that guarantee exactly where it matters most. Reopening should be its own visible, public moment on the same register that shows the count — the same device already used for pile transitions, ideally spoken aloud too.

Scope stays small

Reopening a zone doesn't reopen its closed piles. Reopening a pile doesn't require reopening its zone unless the zone is also closed. Each action does the smallest thing that solves the actual problem.

A pile closed at 87, reopened for a recount, closed again at 89 — every number anyone asks about is answerable straight from the log, nothing edited in place:

#142  system   pile closed · 87×50+120 short = 4,470 kg
#143  operator reopened · "buyer wants to recount, thinks we're short two"
#151  hold     counted · 2+50=52 กก. (standard)
#152  hold     counted · 2+50=52 กก. (standard)
#153  system   pile closed · 89×50+120 short = 4,570 kg

Who can reopen — flagged, not answered. The PRD hasn't specified per-role permissions yet (NFR‑6 already marks this open); whatever role gets to close a sale is the natural candidate to reopen one, but that's this document's opinion, not a decision. A harder, second gap: the buyer isn't in the app (FR‑9.5) — the public register covers a reopen while the ล้ง is standing there, but there's no mechanism at all for their acknowledgment if a pile reopens after they've left. That gap is inherent to the v1 model, worth naming rather than assuming away.

To verify once this is built

QA scenarios

  • Close the last pile in a zone, then the last zone in a sale, back to back, no manual navigation — the "close sale" prompt must be reachable without ever touching the switcher.
  • Close a zone with a pile still Counting — the warning names that exact pile; it shows as Closed immediately after, not orphaned.
  • Reopen that force-closed pile — the zone must not also silently reopen.
  • Try to reopen anything while the sale is Closed — must block, and point at reopening the sale as the actual next step.
  • Reopen, recount, close again — read the log back: original close, reopen with reason, new close, all present, ordered by sequence number, not wall clock (FR‑12.5).
  • Close a sale with one zone untouched — must be allowed, worded honestly, not blocked on an "every zone must be used" rule that doesn't exist.
  • Attempt any reopen with an empty reason — must reject client-side; a blank "why" defeats the audit trail's purpose.

What this means, per discipline

Dev

The state model and transition table are the implementation contract — three independent status fields, exactly the transitions listed, each producing one immutable log event. No fourth status value needed anywhere; resist adding one.

QA

The scenarios above are a starting checklist. The general pattern worth hunting for more of: what happens when a broader scope closes or reopens around a narrower one mid-action — that seam is where the real bugs will cluster.

UI design

Two screens have no prior art in this project: the close-pile decision point (§ journey) and the public reopening moment (§ reopen). Everything else extends a pattern — confirmation panels, the transition card, the event log — already designed and reviewed elsewhere in docs/ux/.
