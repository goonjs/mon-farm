mon-farm · docs/ux · design system draft v0.1

An instrument, not a document.

Two people stand around a scale and watch a number that decides how much money changes hands. The buyer never touches the device. Everything here follows from that one fact.

1 · The three registers

Every element on screen belongs to exactly one register, decided by who it's for and from how far away. If you can't name an element's register, it doesn't belong on the screen.

Public

the buyer · ~10 m

The count. The dial. Accept and reject feedback. Nothing else.

This is a fixed budget and it is already spent. Three digits at the largest numeral a 6.1″ phone can physically produce leave about 35 mm of width for everything else — which is why operator information lives behind tabs.

Operator

the person counting · ~0.4 m

Pile identity, expected reading, running totals, every control, every state indicator.

When someone asks to "just add a small total under the number," the answer is the Pile tab. That is what it is for.

Announced

both parties · by ear

Spoken count, read-back, corrections, pile totals.

Designed here, not bolted on. Every announcement writes an event whether or not audio succeeded — and the screen must be trustworthy in silence, because audio is the likeliest thing to fail.

2 · The bezel sweep

The three-second hold is drawn as a stroke travelling the stage's perimeter. It costs the numeral nothing — which matters, because the numeral's height is the tightest constraint in the product — and the perimeter is the longest path on the screen, so progress stays visible at the distance the buyer is standing. An early release collapses the stroke back to the start, so rejection is as loud as acceptance. This is the one place the system spends any boldness.

42

press and hold · 3 seconds

844 × 390 — reference landscape phone. Numeral is 66% of viewport height at three digits.

3 · Colour

Four signal hues, and no hue does double duty. This is the load-bearing decision in the palette: because the hues are disjoint, the non-colour encodings in §5 agree with colour instead of competing with it.

4 · Contrast, measured

Computed live in this page from the same token values the app ships, by the same formula scripts/check-contrast.mjs uses in CI. Each pairing has a floor chosen for its use — AAA for the count and primary text, AA for secondary, 3:1 for control boundaries per WCAG 1.4.11.

5 · Type

System stacks only. The app must be fully usable offline on first launch — a font that fails to load is a count nobody can read. Thai and Latin share one stack, with Noto Sans Thai ahead of the generic fallback so Thai doesn't render lighter beside Latin.

The public numeral is not in this ramp. It's sized as a fraction of the short viewport dimension, never in points — finding F-9.7 is arithmetic about physical millimetres, and fixing the size in points silently gives back the millimetres the cross-check depends on. There's an automated assertion against exactly that regression.

6 · States that never rely on colour

Five states need non-colour encoding. Each gets a shape, a position and a word; colour is the fourth channel, never the first.

state indicators

  • กำลังนับ Counting
  • หยุด Paused / not ready
  • 🔇 ไม่มีเสียง Audio degraded
  • ⏱ เวลาผิด Clock anomaly
  • ค้าง 12 Unsynced

The last three are non-blocking by design. They report; they never gate counting. A screen that stops you counting because its clock is wrong has misunderstood which of the two matters more.

basket outcomes

✓ นับแล้ว · counted ✗ ไม่นับ · not counted ⚖ สั้น · short basket

A rejected basket is red and struck through and marked ✗ and announced. Any one of those four channels alone carries the meaning — which is what finding F-7 requires, since a silently rejected tap turns a phantom count into a missed count.

controls

Nothing green is pressable and nothing blue reports state. Destructive controls are never adjacent to the stage or to each other, and always take a second confirm.
