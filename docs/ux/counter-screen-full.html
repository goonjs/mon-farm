    How this was built

    UX and QA, working the same list

    Each region below got the same question twice: what does the operator need here, and what happens when someone does the wrong thing here. Where the two pulled against each other, that tension is written down rather than quietly resolved in the code.

    Topbar & hierarchy

    UX — Breadcrumb is a button, not decoration; the switcher it opens is the only place piles are picked, so counting context is never ambiguous. Sync and clock state sit here because they're non-blocking (NFR‑2, FR‑12.7) — informative, never interrupting a weighing.

    QA — Cycle the sync pill through all three states (synced / pending / error) — try it. None of them should be able to block a count. The clock flag is the same: toggle it mid-hold and the hold must keep running. Both are demo-toggleable here specifically so this is checkable without faking real drift or a dropped connection.

    Count tab

    UX — One thing lives here: the hold-to-count ring, validated separately before this page existed. The expected reading is one small line, not a second hero — it's fixed for the whole pile, so it doesn't compete with the number that changes every basket (finding F‑2).

    QA — Three states worth breaking on purpose: hold and release at 49% (must cancel, must log "released early", must not count); hold to 100% and keep holding without releasing (must not double-fire — try it, it won't); switch tabs mid-hold (the hold should abandon cleanly, not leave a ghost ring on return).

    Undo & the short-basket log

    UX — Both are siblings of the counter, not menu items (F‑13, F‑14) — a short basket is common enough, at the end of nearly every pile, that hiding it costs real accuracy. Undo is one tap, no prompt, because it's used in-rhythm; voiding a specific basket is on the Log tab instead, where there's time to give a reason (F‑12).

    QA — Undo must disable itself the instant there's nothing left to undo in the current pile — check it doesn't reach into a different pile's last count after switching context. A short basket still increments the public count (it's a real basket) but must contribute its own logged weight, not the standard 50 kg — confirm the pile total math after logging one, by hand, against what's on screen.

    Pile & Zone tab

    UX — Tare and standard weight are editable only before Ready, then visibly lock (FR‑3.4) — changing a number that money depends on should never be one accidental tap away once baskets are already on record against it.

    QA — Try to edit tare after starting a pile — the stepper must be inert, and the lock note must say why, not just silently ignore the tap. Rollups (zone, sale) must move the instant a basket is counted, not only when a pile closes (FR‑6.4) — watch the numbers on this tab while counting on the other tab.

    Closing a pile

    UX — Closing is a confirmation, not a silent action — it shows the pile's own numbers back before committing, and the next screen is a full-bleed transition card, not a number that quietly resets from 87 to 1 (finding F‑4). The ล้ง should never see the public register do something they can't explain.

    QA — Close a pile with zero baskets counted — it must still work, worded honestly ("0 baskets — close anyway?"), not block the operator from correcting an accidental zone pick. Confirm the transition card requires a tap to dismiss — it should never auto-advance while the ล้ง is mid-read of the closing total.

    Closing a zone

    UX — A closed zone locks its piles against further counting, but its total keeps rolling up into the sale exactly as before — closing is a workflow marker, not a data cutoff.

    QA — this is where the two teams disagreed. UX wanted zone-close to always be one tap, matching pile-close. QA pushed back: closing a zone while one of its piles is still mid-count would strand that pile — counted so far, but with no way back in. The resolution kept here is a warning, not a block: if any pile in the zone is still counting, the confirmation says so by name before letting the tap through. Try closing a zone with an open pile in it and check the warning actually names that pile.

    Two findings this assembly surfaced

    • F-19

      Everything on this page does not fit one phone screen at a size anyone could use standing up — that's why it's tabbed rather than stacked. But FR‑9.1 doesn't stop applying just because the operator is looking at the Log tab, so the count persists as a small pill in the topbar whenever the Count tab isn't active. A tablet has room to show Count and Pile & Zone side by side without tabs at all — worth trying once the phone-vs-tablet question (F‑9.7, open question 2) is settled.

    • F-20

      Closing a zone needed a guard the original findings didn't anticipate: nothing previously covered what happens to a pile still being counted when its zone closes around it. Recommendation implemented here — warn by naming the pile, don't block — keeps the one-tap close from becoming a trap while still making the risk visible.
